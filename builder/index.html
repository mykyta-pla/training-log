Blocks

Any combination. Order is fixed: mobility, cardio, strength, core, finisher.

 

How demand and minutes work

Each block asks for a level of demand and a number of minutes. Demand is a preference read off the movement itself, not a filter: at Hard the block draws the movements that cost you something, and where the pool hasn’t got them it settles for Working rather than coming back empty. Readiness outranks it — a red band caps every block at Light and says so.

The minutes start as shares of the session length, so they scale when you change it, and they have floors: mobility 8, strength 15, cardio 10, core 4, finisher 4. Mobility’s floor is 8 rather than 6 because end-range work needs time under tension — 6 only ever worked when the drills were passive. You can’t allocate more than the session has.

How combining works

Upper + Lower alternates between the two rather than spending the session on whichever came first, so four exercises give two of each. Full body doesn’t combine: picking it clears the rest.

Format

How the order is chosen

Each slot asks for a role as well as a pattern, so a hinge slot wanting a main lift draws a deadlift and not a good morning. One main lift per session and it goes first; after it, coached movements before straightforward ones, so the skill work happens while you’re fresh. Reps come from the movement itself — a deadlift and a good morning don’t share numbers. With less equipment there may be no main lift available, and the slot takes the most substantial thing that fits rather than leaving a hole.

What that does

Movements from your last 2 completed sessions go to the back of the queue. If a slot has nothing else left, it takes the least recent instead of failing.
